# 🐦 Flappy Bird

I made a classic **Flappy Bird** game and learned how to deploy it.

I meticulously followed a tutorial until one bug came along and it all fell off. I spent **3 days** trying to think of a solution and tinkering around, and through that process I learned more about the engine than I did through the tutorial itself.

It made me realise how **one tiny bug could be your gateway to knowledge as a beginner.**

All in all, although game development isn't exactly my forte, I'm glad to have touched it and experienced creating something as nostalgic and exciting as Flappy Bird.

## 🧠 What I Learned

The biggest lesson from this project wasn't necessarily about game development.

It was about **learning through failure and experimentation**.

When the bug appeared, I couldn't simply continue following the tutorial. I had to actually understand what was happening and figure out why the game wasn't behaving as expected.

That meant spending time experimenting, changing things, breaking things, and trying different solutions.

Eventually, I realised that getting stuck can sometimes be more valuable than having everything work immediately.

As a beginner, **a bug can be a gateway to knowledge** because it forces you to look underneath the surface instead of simply following instructions.

---

## 🐛 The Debugging Experience

The bug that I encountered ended up being one of the most useful parts of the project.

What initially felt like something slowing me down became an opportunity to understand the Unity engine better.

Instead of just asking *"How do I fix this?"*, I started asking:

* Why is this happening?
* What is causing it?
* What happens if I change this?
* How does this component actually work?
* Why does the engine behave this way?

That shift from **following** to **understanding** was probably the most valuable thing I took away from the project.
